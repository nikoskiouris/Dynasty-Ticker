# The v4 football calibration and its original price units

This study records the v4 scoring scale. It assigns one point per 3.6137 expected discounted PPR points above a replacement lineup over the current season and the following two. A 50-point player therefore represents about 180.69 points of estimated lineup surplus. The 100-point reference is a fixed historical estimate. Managers can compare assets across refreshes even when the current league leader changes. An individual outcome can exceed the reference.

## Historical construction

The raw football estimate sums remaining-season lineup surplus, next-season surplus at an 85% weight, and third-season surplus at a 72.25% weight. Each season’s replacement lineup enforces 12 teams, 1 QB, 2 RBs, 3 WRs, 1 TE, 1 flex and 1 superflex per team. The observed target uses those same discounts and marginal-lineup rules on actual NFL results. A player who left the league contributes zero in the missed seasons.

We fitted the input-to-outcome curve using two archived preseason boards and two week-13 reconstructions, in 2018 and 2020. The week-13 inputs use only NFL games and roster observations through week 12. The remaining-season replacement level uses results after the cutoff. We linked archived projections to player IDs without requiring future participation. Players with unresolved IDs contribute to the forecast board’s competition for slots and are excluded from outcome fitting. The 2018 and 2020 schedules had 16 games. Their historical schedules drive the observed targets. The scorer uses the deployed modern 17-game annualization assumptions on these reconstructions, which limits cross-era precision. The archived 2018 forecast lacked fumbles and two-point conversions. [August 2018 ESPN stat forecasts](https://github.com/jpurpura/fantasy-football-2018/blob/8dee3651860987af2dfbabace450c80fba70a204/projections/espn_projections_aug_src.xlsx) · [September 2020 FantasyPros stat forecasts](https://github.com/fantasydatapros/data/blob/e36e1de3303de41ab92993cf1d764afdeb117407/fantasypros/fp_projections.csv) · [nflverse NFL outcomes](https://github.com/nflverse/nflverse-data/releases/tag/stats_player)

To calibrate the curve, we grouped positive raw surplus into fixed PPR intervals with upper edges of 10, 30, 60, 100, 160, 240, 360 and 520, plus an open upper tail. Each interval’s mean outcome received 12 observations’ worth of shrinkage toward its mean raw forecast. We pooled any reversed adjacent means to preserve monotonicity. The production curve linearly interpolates the resulting points, anchors zero to zero, and extends the highest fitted ratio into the thin upper tail. Scores stop at 100. The resulting expected-surplus anchors are generated by the manual CLI and stored in `dynasty_calibration.py`.

Using the best *realized* three-year outcomes in 2000–18 would set the 100-point reference at 501.36. That hindsight ceiling put the strongest development forecasts at only 51–79 points. Before evaluating 2023, we instead set the reference to **361.37**. This is the median best *estimated* preseason surplus in the 2018 and 2020 development archives after calibration. Only one player across the four development boards reached the cap. A two-season reference has considerable sampling uncertainty. Its role is to define a fixed display unit.

## Held-out scale and spacing

The 2023 preseason and week-13 boards were held out from scale fitting. Their complete outcome horizon ends in 2025. All fit outcomes end by 2022. Historical market quotes and current player names or prices were excluded from both models. This audit isolates football score spacing. Other components had previously been examined on overlapping seasons, so 2023 is a chronological holdout for this calibration. [September 2023 archived stat projections](https://github.com/Saurabh1975/ffb-streamlit/blob/1bb6d3f9f579c350de2afff862921b755d2c442b/Data/fpros_projections.csv)

The baseline reproduces v3’s independent lineup and future normalization and its board-maximum scaling. The second baseline gives v3 its own best-fit multiplier from the same development outcomes, 3.21984 discounted PPR per v3 point. A third baseline uses the uncalibrated raw forecast directly. All methods are evaluated in identical benchmark units against **uncapped** outcomes. The groups below were determined by the raw forecast at each cutoff. They contain repeated observations of players and are unsuitable for independent-observation significance claims.

We compared tier means by taking the absolute difference between each tier’s mean prediction and outcome, then weighting by tier size. On 2023 this error was **1.05 points for the native scale**, 2.79 for published-scale v3, 4.09 for rescaled v3 and 3.36 for the uncalibrated raw PPR forecast. Across the top 240, unscaled v3 MAE/RMSE was 13.72/21.73, and raw PPR was 13.43/21.50. The native curve improved mean spacing and reduced squared error. It increased absolute error on many bench players because most contribute little while a minority break out. Tight ends were an exception even on squared error, at 13.19 native versus 12.86 for rescaled v3.

For the package comparison at each cutoff, each asset ranked 1–36 was paired against the players ranked 36 and 72 places below it. We compared the **signed difference** in their subsequent additive lineup surpluses. This predetermined pairing checks trade-off spacing without selecting successful deals after the fact. It does not establish how often a manager would accept those offers or evaluate the calculator’s separate package adjustment.

## Forward estimates for players on injured reserve

We checked whether treating missed games as partial exposure improves the estimate of *future seasons* after an injury. For players listed on reserve after week 12, we compared the published estimate with two alternatives. One gave missed games half their normal weight in the current-season sample. The other used only preseason and previous-season evidence. We recalculated the future replacement levels for each board and measured actual surplus in the next two seasons, weighted 1 and 0.85. No trade ranks or current-season lineup points enter the outcome. Roster matches require reserve status in that week's file; named non-injury reserve categories were excluded where available. The 2018 file has less detail about reserve types. [nflverse weekly rosters](https://github.com/nflverse/nflverse-data/releases/tag/weekly_rosters)

Errors are in annual-equivalent PPR surplus. The half-weight alternative improved the development results but worsened both error measures on the held-out reserve cohort. Its mean prediction was closer for the eight held-out QBs, even as individual errors grew. That subgroup mixes players with very different recovery and starting-role outcomes. We retained the published future-production estimator. Reproduce with `python player-values/research/future_blend.py --reserve development` and `--reserve holdout`.
